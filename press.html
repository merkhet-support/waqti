Press kit

Waqti: a habit app for days that don't start at 7 AM.

Waqti fits habits, tasks and prayers around the life people actually live: night shifts and rotations, prayer times, church and Ramadan fasts, bills, birthdays and renewals. It needs no account, and everything stays on the phone.

The story

Most apps think your day starts at 7 and ends at midnight. Yours might start at 7 in the evening. You might pray before dawn, fast with your church, or work four nights on and four off. Waqti is built for that day.

The studio's name, Merkhet, comes from one of the oldest timekeeping tools we know: more than 3,000 years ago, Egyptian astronomers told the hours of the night by the stars, and the merkhet was one of their instruments. Waqti puts that idea in your pocket.

What's different

  • Your day resets while you sleep, not at midnight. A nurse's 2 AM check-in counts for her shift.
  • Prayer reminders that respect sleep. Asleep at Dhuhr? One reminder when you wake, with the time left.
  • Every fast of the year, for every tradition, worked out on the phone for your church or school.
  • Life admin, ready-made: bills that wait until paid, car service by date or kilometres, birthdays with a week's notice, iqama and passport renewals.
  • Safety built in: medicine reminders never pause on sick days; quit trackers point to real help (Egypt's helpline 16023); no health claims.

Questions we're asked

Why both Muslim and Christian calendars in one app?
Because that's what Egypt looks like, and many families and workplaces live by more than one calendar. Each person sees only their own faith's reminders, and faith features are optional.
Do you give health or medical advice?
No. Waqti sends reminders. Wherever health comes up (medicine during Ramadan, quitting alcohol or painkillers, the period tracker), it says plainly to ask a doctor or pharmacist, and it makes no health claims.
Where does my data go?
Nowhere. There's no account and no Waqti server. Data stays on the phone and in the phone's own backup. Shared family lists are end-to-end encrypted and travel as links the user sends.
